# Product

BillShield helps U.S. adults aged 25–45 who pay their own residential internet bills and have little time to review recurring charges. The initial problem is understanding fees and preparing a concrete provider conversation, not searching imaginary competitor offers.

The workflow: start a private guest session → upload PDF or manually enter charges → review all extracted fields → acknowledge any reconciliation gap → confirm → analyze → select an evidence-backed recommendation → use a personalized script → record provider response and confirmed recurring outcome → return next month.

The value is clarity, useful questions and an honest record of what changed. The user remains in control. The initial scope excludes bank connections, automated negotiations, guarantees and unsolicited plan changes. Human verification and deterministic integer-cent calculations are the primary trust mechanisms.

The proposed free tier offers one bill analysis and one script. Plus at $7.99/month is a pricing hypothesis, with ten analyses monthly, longer history, reminders, comparisons and exports envisioned. No demand, willingness to pay, conversion rate, traction or partnership is claimed. The hackathon guest experience permits extra bills to demonstrate persistence and comparison under safety quotas; it is not a working paid subscription.

Retention hypothesis: review the next statement, detect changes, confirm promotion dates, follow up on open actions and measure recurring reductions. North Star: user-confirmed annualized savings (explicitly self-attested). Future measurements include upload completion, analysis-to-action, repeat upload and cohort retention. These metrics have not been measured with customers.

Differentiation: bill-grounded evidence and concrete provider questions rather than generic advice or a chat-first experience. No invented discounts; one-time fees and recurring reductions stay separate. Market size and business potential remain unvalidated hypotheses until customer discovery and paid conversion tests are run.
